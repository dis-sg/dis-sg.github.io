<h2>Stars remains the most common graphic element in NDP logos 🤩</h2>

Singapore turns 61 this year, and the National Day Parade logo proudly highlights this. While chatter about National Day graphics have largely been about <a href="https://www.designinsingapore.com/snapshot-localisation-ai/" target="_blank">grassroots banners</a> made using AI (and not using AI like grammar checks), the logo quietly continues a 30-year tradition of national branding, aligning each look to the birthday theme and design trends. 

Readers of this blog may remember a deep dive series of posts in 2020 sorting NDP logos by <a href="https://www.designinsingapore.com/NDP-Logos-sorted-1/" target="_blank">graphical elements</a>, <a href="https://www.designinsingapore.com/NDP-Logos-sorted-2/" target="_blank">colour schemes</a> and <a href="https://www.designinsingapore.com/NDP-Logos-sorted-3/" target="_blank">typography</a>. This post will explore how this year’s logo stacks up against previous ones.

But before we do that, let’s learn more about this year’s logo and theme.

<h2>Theme: Majulah Singapura, Go Beyond!</h2>

<blockquote>
“Majulah Singapura” captures our Singapore Spirit and our enduring aspirations to keep moving forward and progress.
<br>
“Go Beyond!” is our call to action to do better for Singapore and Singaporeans, and to show care and concern for one another and our wider community.
</blockquote>

This year’s theme is very similar to last year’s, which was simply “Majulah Singapura”. As last year was SG60, the “60” in the logo was shaped like “go”. Even so, the theme is fitting for Singapore's 61st birthday, given how we are <i>going beyond</i> our diamond jubilee.

“Majulah” was also featured in the NDP themes of 2022, 2015 and 2011.

<h2>NDP 2026 Logo</h2>

Comprising the NDP theme, Singapore’s age and the five stars from Singapore’s flag, the logo’s few elements manage to convey the theme in a cohesive and organised manner. The logo was designed by local social media agency <a href="https://www.instagram.com/tmrmediasg/" target="_blank">TMR Media</a>.

The “61” represents 61 years of independence. The forward leaning “1” is seamlessly shaped as an arrow, a visualisation of our constant strive for progress and excellence.

The five stars, arranged in an ascending arc, represent our nation’s ideals of democracy, peace, progress, justice, and equality and serve as the guiding force behind our collective progress.

<h2>How Unique is the NDP 2026 Logo?</h2>

NDP logos broadly fall into three different categories when comparing graphical elements: flag elements (crescent moon, stars), human anatomy (heart, hands, human-like figures) and geography (silhouette of Singapore, water).

Given how stars are easy to include in logos, it is no surprise that the vast majority of NDP logos feature them. Sometimes, they play an integral part in logos, while in other cases, they are sprinkled on as visual garnish.

<figure>
<img src="https://i.imgur.com/VdPCnGW.png" alt="NDP logos stars">
<figcaption>Note: This collection does not include logos with the canton (crescent moon and five stars as arranged in the flag)</figcaption>
</figure>

The NDP 2026 logo features its five stars prominently, which helps viewers make that NDP connection as opposed to an Air Force anniversary or F1 logo.

The logo’s ascending arc is also in good company. Recent NDP logos had designs evoking upward momentum, including in 2020, 2022 and 2023.

<figure>
<img src="https://i.imgur.com/5W9pRyT.png" alt="NDP logos upward momentum">
<figcaption>Note: This collection only includes logos with a prominent upward momentum in one direction</figcaption>
</figure>

Moving on to typography - this year’s logo features so few elements that it is now included in a rare collection of logos that do not mention “NDP”. As mentioned earlier, the stars really do the heavy lifting.

<figure>
<img src="https://i.imgur.com/Lq7jTPW.png" alt="NDP logos no ndp">
</figure>

Another rare collection this year’s logo is added to is the one that does not indicate the year. It seems to be a trend this decade, with half of these logos omitting it. Viewers are still able to decipher which year the logo corresponds to as all these logos (sans the 2015 and 1999 ones) feature stylised numbers of Singapore’s age. 

<figure>
<img src="https://i.imgur.com/PeVhROb.png" alt="NDP logos year">
</figure>

Interestingly, Singapore’s age has been featured in each NDP logo since 2021 (although you probably have to squint to see the “57” for NDP 2022). 

<figure>
<img src="https://i.imgur.com/GlsIm7Q.png" alt="NDP logos age">
</figure>

Overall, NDP 2026’s logo stands out for packing as much information possible in as few elements. Compared to its predecessors, it may not stand out as much, but it is still a solid entry which fits its theme well.
